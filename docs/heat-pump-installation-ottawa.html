Is a heat pump right for your home?

A heat pump moves heat instead of burning fuel to make it. In summer it works like an air conditioner. In winter it runs in reverse and pulls heat from the outdoor air, even when it's well below freezing.

For most Ottawa homes the real question isn't whether a heat pump works. It's which setup makes sense for your house and budget:

Most popular

Dual-fuel

A heat pump plus your existing gas furnace for the coldest days. Best when the furnace still has life left.

All-electric

A cold-climate heat pump with electric backup heat, for homes without gas or owners who want to drop it.

Ductless mini-split

For homes without ductwork, additions, or a cold room over the garage.

Heat pump questions we hear most

How much does a heat pump cost in Ottawa?

For a typical detached home, a ducted cold-climate heat pump usually costs roughly $9,000 to $16,000 installed, before any rebates. It depends on the size of the home, the ductwork and whether the electrical panel needs work. We give a fixed quote after a home visit.

Will a heat pump keep my house warm at −25°C?

A properly sized cold-climate model keeps producing heat at those temperatures, just less efficiently. Most Ottawa installs keep the existing furnace or add electric backup for the handful of very cold nights.

Can I keep my gas furnace?

Yes. A dual-fuel (hybrid) setup uses the heat pump most of the year and switches to the furnace when it's very cold. It's the most common choice for Ottawa homes that already have a working furnace.

Are there rebates?

Federal, provincial and utility programs change often, so we check what's currently available for your home during the quote visit and help you with the application.
